SCOPE OF PROPOSAL The South Carolina Retirement System Investment Commission1 is soliciting proposals from qualified investment consulting firms with substantial experience advising institutional investors to provide strategic asset allocation, investment policy review, performance reporting, and research services to the South Carolina Retirement System Portfolio (“Portfolio”), which is invested throughout many asset classes and diversified strategies. More detailed descriptions of these services are set forth in Section III. This is not a full retainer general consulting mandate. RSIC intends to retain other consultants who will have primary oversight of the Retirement System’s alternative investments, including private equity, private debt, and real assets. The selected consultant will report to the Commission and will be expected to work with other RSIC consultants, its master custodial bank (currently, The Bank of New York Mellon (“BNY”)), and its fund administrator (currently, Conifer Financial Services (“Conifer”)) in order to provide timely and thorough services to the Commission with regard to the Portfolio as a whole. Offerors that meet the minimum requirements outlined in Section 3.2.0 and would like to provide the requested consulting services, as more fully described in Section III, may submit proposals for consideration. The Commission will select the most qualified firm based on the response to this RFP, meetings at consultants’ offices, and presentations by the top candidates to the Commission. All Proposals must be complete and carefully worded and must convey all of the information requested in order to be considered responsive. If a Proposal fails to conform to the essential requirements of the RFP or any deadlines are not met, only the Contact will be the judge as to whether that variance is significant enough to consider the RFP nonresponsive or non-qualified and therefore not considered for award. The resulting contract from this RFP, if any, will be between the selected consultant and RSIC. II. INTRODUCTION 2.1.0 Background Information. The Commission is responsible for investing and managing all assets held in trust for the participants and beneficiaries of five (5) governmental defined benefit plans: South Carolina Retirement System, South Carolina Police Officers Retirement System, Retirement System for Judges and Solicitors of the State of South Carolina, Retirement System for Members of the General Assembly of the State of South Carolina, and the National Guard Retirement System, which are collectively referred to hereinafter as the “Retirement System”. Effective October 1, 2005, the State Retirement System Preservation and Investment Reform Act (Act 153) established the Commission and devolved fiduciary responsibility for all investments of the Retirement System upon the Commission. Seven individuals sit on the Commission, including the State Treasurer and the Executive Director of the South Carolina Public Employee Benefit Authority, who is a non-voting member. Act 153 also provided that equity investments cannot exceed 70 percent of the total investment Portfolio and created the position of Chief Investment Officer (“CIO”). Section 9-16-315. “South Carolina Retirement System Investment Commission” or “RSIC” refers to the agency established by South Carolina law for the purpose of investing and managing all assets held in trust for the participants and beneficiaries of the state’s five separate defined benefit plans. 7 [PDF page 8] South Carolina Retirement System Investment Commission General Investment Consulting Services RFP members of the Commission are: Rebecca Gunnlaugsson, Ph.D, Chair; Ron Wilder, Ph.D, Vice Chair; Reynolds Williams; Peggy Boykin, CPA; Allen R. In November 2006, a constitutional amendment allowing for full diversification of the Retirement System’s Portfolio was approved in a statewide referendum and subsequently ratified by the General Assembly in February of 2007. Since ratification, the Commission has transitioned to a more diversified asset allocation. The Retirement System had assets of approximately $28 billion as of July of 2016. RSIC’s primary investment objective is to provide, over the long-term, an adequate pool of assets to support the benefit obligations to participants and beneficiaries of the Retirement System. A secondary objective is to reduce, over time, the unfunded liability of the Retirement System. In pursuing these objectives, RSIC seeks to achieve a high level of investment return consistent with a prudent level of portfolio risk. The assets of the Retirement System are invested in conformity with the requirements of South Carolina law, and the Portfolio is designed to increase the probability of achieving those long-term objectives. RSIC manages a complex and diversified Portfolio that includes traditional as well as alternative asset classes. Alternative asset classes used include private equity, private debt, private real estate, hedge funds, global asset allocation, and opportunistic credit. RSIC gains access to these asset classes through traditional means as well as through several strategic partnerships. 2.2.0 Additional Background Information. SCOPE OF SERVICES, DELIVERABLES, AND MINIMUM QUALIFICATIONS 3.1.0 Scope of Services and Key Deliverables. The scope of services (“Scope of Services”) for the selected Consultant (“Consultant”) includes the following consulting services as related to the development and oversight of the Portfolio: 3.1.1 Asset Allocation and Asset Liability Modeling. Consultant will support the Commission by providing asset allocation and asset liability modeling for the Portfolio, including, but not limited to, the following: a) Assist in establishing the strategic asset allocation for the Commission, including investment objectives and risk parameters for the Portfolio; 8 [PDF page 9] South Carolina Retirement System Investment Commission General Investment Consulting Services RFP b) Using the most up-to-date capital market assumptions, analyze and quantify the risk/return parameters for the Portfolio at least annually; c) At least annually, or if requested by the Commission in response to a material change in the investment environment, legislation, or actuarial inputs affecting the Portfolio, review the existing strategic asset allocation, and recommend changes, if appropriate; d) At least every three years, or if requested by the Commission in response to a material change in the investment environment, legislation, or actuarial inputs affecting the Portfolio, conduct a comprehensive asset liability analysis for the purpose of assessing the impact on the Portfolio’s asset allocation and risk management decisions; e) Test the impact of adding or eliminating asset classes and submit reports related to such analysis; and f) Ratify benchmarks used to monitor performance. 3.1.2 Review and Evaluation of the Portfolio. Consultant will prepare comprehensive quarterly reports of the investment performance of the total Portfolio by manager, asset class, and total fund, including benchmark and peer universe comparisons and present them to the Commission at a scheduled Commission meeting. The Consultant will also produce a separate Executive Summary Report to accompany the comprehensive report. The Consultant must be able to maintain a satisfactory electronic interface with RSIC’s custodial bank through an online connection at the firm’s expense. 3.1.3 Annual Portfolio Review. Consultant will annually provide a comprehensive review of the Portfolio, including, but not limited to, quantitative factors, such performance versus relevant benchmarks and peers and applicable risk metrics, and qualitative factors, such as strength and effectiveness of implementation strategy. 3.1.4 Policy Review. Consultant will annually review RSIC’s investment policies and provide advice based on current investment management practice and theory. 3.1.5 Other Required Services. Consultant will provide ad hoc research and miscellaneous services, including, but not limited to, the following: a) Ongoing advice and counsel to the Commission; b) Access to all off-the-shelf research; and c) Special projects at the Commission’s request. 3.1.6 Public Markets Advisory Services. RSIC may determine that advice for certain public markets advisory services should be included in this mandate. As used in this document, “public markets” encompasses all portions of the Portfolio other than (a) private equity, (b) private debt, and (c) private market real assets. Accordingly, in addition to traditional equity and fixed income mandates, Consultant would be expected to provide advisory services to the Commission regarding investments in areas, including, but not limited to, commodities, hedged strategies, listed infrastructure, and REITs, as well as investments and/or implementation strategies that may not fall neatly into the foregoing public versus private framework. For these public market investments, Consultant would be expected to: a) Provide access to asset class research and investment manager due diligence write-ups, as requested; and 9 [PDF page 10] South Carolina Retirement System Investment Commission General Investment Consulting Services RFP b) Provide the Commission and Staff with an opinion of public markets manager recommendations made by RSIC’s staff (“Staff”). These reports should include a review of the recommendation, an assessment of the quality of the investment manager, and a determination of whether the recommendation is in accordance with the Portfolio’s strategic goals. Capabilities should be outlined as a response to the corresponding questions in the RFP Questionnaire, which is found in Exhibit C – RFP Questionnaire. The cost of this service should not be included in the primary pricing for this mandate. Separate “add-on” pricing for the inclusion of this service should be provided in the Fee Proposal, which is attached to this RFP as Exhibit D – Fee Proposal. 3.1.7 General Provisions Relating to the Scope of Services. a) Consultant will attend all investment-related Commission meetings. Meetings generally occur on the third or fourth Thursday of February, April, June, September, and November of each year. Upon reasonable request, Consultant is also expected to attend, either in person or via teleconference, ad hoc or special meetings that may occur throughout the year. While the Commission has averaged two to three special meetings per year, not all of these meetings involve investment-related issues. All meetings will take place in South Carolina. The Commission’s offices (where the vast majority of meetings have taken place over the last five years) are located in Columbia, South Carolina. b) Consultant may, from time to time, be asked to participate in meetings with the legislative committees that oversee the Commission and the Retirement System, the co-trustees of the Retirement System, or other stakeholder groups. All meetings will take place in South Carolina. c) Other Meetings with the Commission: From time to time, Consultant may be required to meet with the Commission at a place of their choosing within South Carolina upon reasonable notice. d) Communications with the Commission: Whenever Consultant is obligated to seek input from the Commission, make a recommendation to the Commission, or otherwise communicate with the Commission, Consultant shall communicate only with the Commission or agents or designees of the Commission unless otherwise directed by the Commission. e) From time to time, the Commission and/or Staff requires technical assistance in responding to inquiries from various parties. Consultant will provide technical assistance to Staff in a timely manner and will provide access to a principal consultant and a primary alternate consultant for technical assistance. Such technical assistance may be required to be furnished in writing. Such assistance may include, but not be limited to, the gathering of market data, the completion of Freedom of Information Act requests, and/or the completion of surveys. f) Consultant recognizes that part of its fiduciary obligation is to bring to the Commission’s attention, through its Chair or Vice-Chair, any information which a reasonable fiduciary would believe to be worthy of further inquiry by the Commission and which relates to the conduct or plans of a Commission employee, agent, a Commissioner, or would otherwise reasonably warrant the Commission’s attention under the circumstances. 3.2.0 Minimum Qualifications. 10 [PDF page 11] South Carolina Retirement System Investment Commission General Investment Consulting Services RFP 3.2.1 Qualifications.
