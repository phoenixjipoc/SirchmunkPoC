Background Information The Anchorage Police and Fire Retirement System (APFRS) was created by the Anchorage Municipal Assembly by ordinance in 1977 (AO No.77-205; AO No. 84-69; AO No. 2000- 65, § 3, 4-18-00). Police and fire employees hired July 1, 1968, or later retroactively became members in the retirement plan. The system is comprised of three retirement plans including a surplus benefit plan, all of which were created for the purpose of encouraging qualified personnel to enter and remain in the service of the Municipality of Anchorage as police officers and firefighters through the establishment of a system of retirement, disability and death benefits for members, and their beneficiaries. The retirement plans are closed with Plan I closing to new members effective June 30, 1977, Plan II closing to new members effective April 17, 1984, and Plan III closing to new members effective May 24, 1994, for new command police and fire, July 17, 1994, for union firefighters and all new union police officers October 5, 1994. A nine-member Board of Trustees governs APFRS. The Board has sole and exclusive fiduciary responsibility over the assets of the retirement system. The Board has sole and exclusive responsibility to administer APFRS in a manner that will assure prompt delivery of benefits and related services to the members and their beneficiaries. The APFRS Director has the responsibility for the overall management and administration of the system in accordance with the direction, policy and goals set by the Board and Anchorage Municipal Code. Reporting to the Director is a staff with the primary responsibility to assist the Director to carry out these policies and goals. An annual actuarial valuation commissioned by the Board determines contributions into the APFRS Plans. The Retirement System is also subject to an annual independent audit commissioned by the Board. The results of the actuarial valuation and audit are provided to the Board, Municipal Executives, and Municipal Assembly. 5 [PDF page 6] B. Police and Fire employees hired July 1, 1968 or later retroactively became members in the retirement plan. The system is comprised of three retirement plans, all of which were created for the purpose of encouraging qualified personnel to enter and remain in the service of the Municipality of Anchorage as police officers and firefighters through the establishment of a system of retirement, disability and death benefits for members, and their beneficiaries. The retirement plans are closed with Plan I closing to new members effective June 30, 1977, Plan II closing to new members effective April 17, 1984, and Plan III closing to new members effective May 24, 1994 for new command police and fire, July 17, 1994 for union firefighters and all new union police officers October 5, 1994. A nine member Board of Trustees governs APFRS. The Board has sole and exclusive fiduciary responsibility over the assets of the retirement system. The Board has the sole and exclusive responsibility to administer APFRS in a manner that will assure prompt delivery of benefits and related services to the members and their beneficiaries. The APFRS Director has the responsibility for the overall management and administration of the system in accordance with the direction, policy and goals set by the Board and Anchorage Municipal Code. Reporting to the Director is a staff with the primary responsibility to assist the Director to carry out these policies and goals. An annual actuarial valuation commissioned by the Board determines contributions into the APFRS Plans. The growth of the Fund results from a combination of employer and employee contributions plus investment gains, less the combination of administrative costs, investment expenses, and benefit payments. The Retirement System is also subject to an annual outside audit commissioned by the Board. The results of the actuarial valuation and audit are provided to the Board, Municipal Executives, and Assembly. [PDF page 27] THE PURPOSE OF THE INVESTMENT POLICY AND OBJECTIVES: 1. Articulate the Board’s views on APFRS’ investment objectives and risk tolerance for the investment portfolio. 2. Establish performance standards to measure the progress towards achieving the objectives. 3. Formulate the Board’s policies and guidelines on: a) asset allocation and diversification of investments; b) allowable investments; c) rebalancing; d) implementing policy-based initiatives; e) monitoring and evaluating the performance and risk of the investment portfolio; and f) investment manager selection and termination. I. INVESTMENT GOAL: 1. Provide for present and future benefit payments including increased benefits should reserves exceed 116%. The overall purpose is to invest pension assets, solely in the interest of providing benefits to the members and their beneficiaries, attempting to minimize the employer and employee contributions and defraying administrative costs. The investment of the fund assets made in accordance with the investment policy described herein are expected to accomplish the goal of maintaining adequate funding of AFPRS’ liabilities over time. The goal of the Board is to design an investment portfolio that will achieve an actuarial rate of return assumption of 6.90%. 2. Make Prudent Investments – With care, skill, prudence and diligence the Board will strive to produce an investment return based on levels of liquidity and investment risk that are prudent and reasonable under present circumstances. Such circumstances may change over time. 3. Diversify the Assets – The Board will diversify the investments of the Fund to maximize the investment return within acceptable investment risk. 4. Establish policy and review process – Annually, APFRS will conduct a formal review of its Investment Policy and will consider long term financial projections prepared by the Investment Consultant. II. INVESTMENT PHILOSOPHY: In general, research has shown that the level of investment return is related to the corresponding level of investment risk. To achieve the actuarial target earning goal, the Board has adopted a long-term strategic asset allocation plan. APFRS will utilize some level of active investment management strategy to provide additional return required to achieve the target earnings rate. The Board strives 3 [PDF page 28] to seek a balance between the certainties of obtaining a set return over time with the risks inherent in higher investment returns. The Board may employ lower cost and lower risk strategies to achieve its stated earnings goals III. INVESTMENT OBJECTIVES: It is in the best interest of the Retirement System that investment objectives be established by the Board for the total fund and for each investment manager. It is clearly understood that these objectives are to be viewed over the long-term and have been established after full consideration of all factors set out in this Statement of Investment Policy (“SIP”). The investment objectives are as follows: A. Total Fund Investment Objectives 1. To achieve a minimum return of 5.0% in excess of the U.S. City Average, All Urban Consumers (CPI-U) as reported by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. However, the return shall be no less than the stated return the actuary utilizes in determining future normal benefit costs. 2. Exceed the return of a custom index, based on the target asset mix specified in Section V.D. 3. The Board shall strive to achieve a level of 116% funding (the Contingency Reserve) of the retirement system’s pension benefit liability in each Plan over time. Also, in each Plan, the Board will annually set aside 25% of any identified surpluses as an additional contingency reserve as required by Municipal Code. The Board has the authority with a simple majority vote to distribute the surplus in accordance with AMC 3.85. 4. Surpluses above the additional contingency reserve may be available for enhanced benefits only upon concurrence and recommendation of the Board's actuary and approval by a Super-Majority of the Retirement Board and by ordinance approved by the Anchorage Assembly. B. The Investment Managers Agreement may also contain additional investment performance objectives and goals for performance monitoring. IV. BOARD AND CONTRACTOR RESPONSIBILITIES: The Board, under the Anchorage Municipal Code Chapter 3.85, is responsible for the establishment of investment policies that guide investment of the Anchorage Police and Fire Retirement System assets. As such: A. The Board Shall determine the appropriate investment policies by considering the implications of a wide range of financial assumptions and [PDF page 29] investment risk; B. The Board is the sole authority for hiring, retaining, and releasing managers, investment consultant and the master custodian; C. The managers shall be responsible to the Board for carrying out the Board’s investment goals, objectives and policies; D. The managers are accorded full discretion, within investment policy limits, to select individual securities and diversify assets under their control. Manager discretion may also be limited by the terms of the investment management agreement between the System and the manager; E. The master custodian shall be responsible for safekeeping assets under the master custodian’s supervision and shall be responsible for administering any Securities Lending Agreement between the Board and the bank; F. The Director shall be responsible for implementing the Board’s investment policies, monitoring the managers and master custodian, and deploying assets within the established asset allocation parameters. G. The Investment Consultant shall review and evaluate the performance of investment managers for APFRS and provide written quarterly reports and advice to the Board. The Investment Consultant shall attend regular periodic meetings and make oral presentations regarding the investment managers’ performance during each period. The Investment Consultant shall conduct an annual review of APFRS investment objectives, guidelines and procedures and, if necessary, make recommendations for amendments thereto. When necessary, assist the Board with interim changes to the System’s Asset Allocation Policy when managers are either added or deleted. V. INVESTMENT POLICIES: A. Actuarially Required Return The assets of the Anchorage Police and Fire Retirement System shall be invested to provide an actuarially required return on investment within acceptable risk parameters, as specified in V.C.4 below, (Acceptable Investment Risk). B. Prudent Person Rule [PDF page 30] The Board of Trustees is by law collectively and individually, considered to be fiduciaries of the System and shall discharge its responsibilities solely in the interest of the members and beneficiaries of the System for the exclusive purpose of providing benefits to the members and their beneficiaries and to defray the reasonable expenses of the System. The Trustees shall exercise those fiduciary responsibilities with the care, skill, prudence and diligence under the circumstances then prevailing that a prudent person acting in a like capacity and familiar with such matters would use in the conduct of an enterprise of a similar character and with similar aims. C. Asset Allocation Factors The Board shall carefully consider the following factors in establishing a long-term asset allocation program: 1. Retirement System Characteristics: a. The system is a public system with tax-exempt status, and investments shall be made in a fiduciary responsible manner for the benefit of the membership. b. It is understood by the Board that member and municipal contributions may be required from time to time. However, the primary source of revenue for benefit payments is from investment earnings on the Retirement System’s assets. c. Plan member demographics and nature of the Plans’ liabilities will be considered when developing an investment strategy. Additionally with APFRS being a closed system, the Board will take into consideration time horizons and invest in more conservative investments over time. 2. Liquidity Requirements: The System’s assets shall be structured to meet the liquidity requirements to pay benefits in a timely manner. Accordingly, the managers should be prepared to provide assets to meet liquidity requirements as the need arises. In addition, investment manager fees benefit payments and expenses will, at the discretion of the Director, be deducted directly from all portfolios. It is important that each manager maintain sufficient liquidity to cover these periodic withdrawals. 3. Quarterly Allocation Rebalancing: The Director, in conjunction with the Fund’s Investment Consultant, will review the asset allocation quarterly and compare it to the recommended target allocation. When one particular manager’s portfolio is over-weighted versus their target allocation, the Director, after coordinating with the Investment Consultant, may reallocate the [PDF page 31] “overage” required by the allocation model and report that activity to the Board at the next ensuing meeting. An ongoing exception is made for alternative asset classes, which may differ from the target ranges outlined for periods of time, due to the illiquid nature of the asset classes. In the event the existing allocation is within the maximum/minimum guidelines (see V. D. below) no action may be taken. The Board will review the overall fund allocation of assets at their annual review meeting. Significantly over-weighted asset classes may be cut back at that time, particularly those which fall outside the guideline allocations. Otherwise, the Board will rely on the advice of the Investment Consultant for changes to weightings or to the Asset Allocation model. 4. Acceptable Investment Risk: The aggregate risk level appropriate for the retirement system is specified in this investment policy statement, which reflects the Fund’s characteristics, and liquidity requirements, keeping in mind the fiduciary purpose for which the assets are accumulated. The acceptable risk level is specified in Section V. D. 5. Asset Allocation Studies: Periodic asset allocation studies may be considered annually by the Board which will: a. Develop a long range rationale to guide the deployment of contributions and assets; b. Identify appropriate expected risk/return parameters for the investment of System assets; and c. Reassess the current investment policy as it pertains to the relationship between the System’s assets and liabilities. 6. Capital Market Assumptions: Capital market assumptions are reviewed annually by the Board and the Investment Consultant. D. Minimum Manager Requirements: In order to be considered as a manager for the system a firm must meet the following minimum standards: 1. The firm must have at least $500 million under management; 2. The firm’s portfolio must have a 5-year investment performance history. F. Manager Guidelines: 1. Managers shall have full discretionary authority in the selection and retention of investments, subject to the provision of law, trust agreements, this Statement of Investment Policy as amended from time to time and investment management agreement between the System and the manager. 2. The responsibility for security selection shall lie with the managers. The managers, however, shall operate within these investment guidelines which shall conform to applicable governmental regulations, and shall cover four specific areas: (a) diversification; (b) credit quality; (c) restricted transactions; and (d) exceptions. a. Diversification: (1) No individual security of the portfolio shall constitute more than the greater of 5% or the security’s weight in the agreed upon market index plus 2%. Other exceptions to this provision may be requested as provided below in section (d). (2) No security shall be bought unless it is traded on one of the major security exchanges or the Over- The-Counter (OTC) market. Listed American Depository Receipts (ADR’s) shall be an approved investment, and shall constitute no more than 20% of the market value of the assets controlled by a manager. (3) Exchange listed derivatives may be included in a manager’s portfolio. The manager is required to provide the Board with a list and explanation of the types of derivatives they expect to invest in, the purposes for holding these securities in their portfolio, the likely portfolio percentage allocation to derivatives and a description of the risks associated with these investments. Collateralized mortgage securities or mortgage-backed securities are authorized by the Board, 9 [PDF page 34] but individual issues must have a Flow Uncertainty Index (“FLUX”) score of 15 or less. b. Credit quality: (1) Commercial paper must be rated P-1 and/or A-1 by Moody’s and Standard & Poor’s respectively. (2) The total bond portfolio will maintain a minimum average credit rating of “BBB+” as rated by both Moody’s and Standard & Poor’s. For Purposes of compliance with this Policy, the manager will adhere to the same ratings scale and policy as that employed by Bloomberg in the structure of the Bloomberg US Aggregate Index (utilizing ratings by Moody’s, S & P and Fitch). c. Portfolio Duration: Total portfolio weighted average duration should not exceed 120% of the bond market’s duration utilizing the Bloomberg US Aggregate Index. d. Restricted transactions: (1) Conflict of interest and transactions between a fiduciary and parties in interest, as described in Sections 406 and 408 of Title I, Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA), are prohibited which provisions are adopted for use in this System. (2) No transactions shall be made which might threaten the tax exempt status of the system. (3) No letter stocks shall be purchased. (4) No funds shall be borrowed. (5) No short sales shall be made. (6) No tax-exempt bond shall be purchased or retained unless, on a comparable quality and duration basis, it provides an expected return superior to that of a taxable bond. (7) No private placements shall be purchased. However, Rule 144A securities with registration rights are permitted investments. (8) No non-exchange-listed derivatives shall be purchased or sold by any of the System’s managers. 10 [PDF page 35] e. Exceptions to Manager Guidelines: (9) The Board acknowledges that when it is in the best interests of the System to invest in a collective vehicle (i.e. comingled fund or mutual fund); it is not possible to dictate specific investment prohibitions. Therefore, it shall be the duty of the manager of such commingled fund to report to the Board on a periodic basis, the extent to which the investment strategies differ from the Board’s SIP. Periodically, the Board will review the differences and determine if the commingled fund remains in the best interest of the System. (10) The managers may request exceptions to the restrictions outlined in V.F.2. by written request to the Board. (11) All exceptions will be submitted in writing to the Board who will, after reviewing and receiving advice from the Fund’s Investment Consultant, render a decision with regard to the request at the next board meeting. 3. Brokerage Transactions The managers shall purchase or sell securities through those firms offering the best price and execution. The Board reserves the right to institute a Brokerage Recapture Program or to use the services of a “Transition Management” brokerage firm if the Board believes this is in the best interest. The decision to use a third party will be based on advice and recommendations from the Board’s Investment Consultant. 11 [PDF page 36] G. MANAGER PERFORMANCE STANDARDS Over the long-term (as defined in the following paragraph) each manager retained to actively manage a portfolio will be expected to outperform on a net of fee basis an appropriately defined and mutually agreed upon market index and to perform (gross of fees) in the top two-fifths (2/5) of managers with similar investment styles as measured by one of the prevailing investment performance measurement services. The time period for measuring manager performance is a market cycle or three to five years, whichever period is shorter. However, if a manager experiences significant organizational changes and/or its performance falls significantly below market averages, the performance of a group of managers of similar style, and its individual performance objectives prior to completion of a market cycle, then the manager’s performance will be evaluated over a shorter time horizon. Over the life of the portfolio, each manager is expected to maintain a portfolio with consistent fundamental portfolio characteristics. H. MONITORING AND EVALUATING MANAGER PERFORMANCE The monitoring and evaluating the total fund and individual managers’ performance will be performed by an Investment Consultant. The objective and purpose of this monitoring is to maintain adequate control over the System’s assets and the managers without stifling their initiative. The Board shall conduct a formal evaluation of each manager’s performance quarterly. These evaluations will focus on adherence to investment policies and the opportunities available in the investment markets. Particular attention will be directed to evaluating performance relative to risk; this will be achieved by comparing individual managers and total Fund performance on a risk-adjusted basis. It is believed that the performance expectations set forth in this SIP are reasonable and consistent in the long run. Actions to be taken as a result of performance reviews will be in accordance with the Retirement Board’s Investment Monitoring and Transition Process Procedure in Exhibit A. Notwithstanding the performance measures in Exhibit A, the Board retains the authority to terminate any manager whenever it is deemed, in the Board’s exercise of its fiduciary duty, to be in the best interest of the System and its participants. 12 [PDF page 37] I.
