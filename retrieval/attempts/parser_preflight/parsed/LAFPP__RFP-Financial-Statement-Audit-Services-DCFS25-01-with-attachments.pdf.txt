1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
The City of Los Angeles Fire & Police Pensions (“LAFPP” or the “Plan”) is seeking 
proposals from qualified certified public accounting firms to conduct annual audits of 
LAFPP’s financial statements for the fiscal years ending June 30, 2025, 2026, and 
2027. 
Firms submitting proposals must have previous experience in auditing large Public 
Pension/Retirement Systems with at least $5 billion in plan net assets. LAFPP intends 
to award a three-year contract with a maximum fee proposal for each fiscal year. The 
Board of Fire and Police Pension Commissioners (“Board”) may choose to exercise its 
authority to extend this relationship beyond the three-year contract term through two 
one-year renewals. Please provide pricing on a three-year basis. The contract will be 
awarded in accordance with the terms and conditions listed in the Request for Proposal 
(RFP). 
The State of California requires all California Public Retirement Systems to furnish 
audited financial statements to the State Controller annually under Sections 7501-7504 
of the Government Code. Audited financial statements are also required under the City 
of Los Angeles Charter Section 362. 
To assist your firm in estimating the level of effort required to render an opinion on the 
compliance requirements, the Minimum Audit Requirements and Reporting Guidelines 
for Public Retirement Systems issued by the State of California Controller’s Office are 
available at https://www.sco.ca.gov/ard_local_rep_audit_guidelines_uas.html.
1.2 Background
LAFPP is one of three pension systems for the City of Los Angeles (the “City”). LAFPP 
administers the pension and retiree health subsidy plans of the City of Los Angeles’ 
sworn firefighters and police officers, including the police officers employed by the Los 
Angeles World Airports and Port of Los Angeles. The Board of Fire and Police Pension 
Commissioners governs LAFPP in accordance with Los Angeles Charter §1106, 
consistent with Article XVI, Section 17 of the California Constitution. The Board consists 
of nine members. Five commissioners are appointed by the Mayor and four are elected 
by the active and retired members of the Fire Department and the Police Department 
(two from each department).
The Plan is a defined benefit single-employer pension plan that operates under the

provisions of Charter Sections 1200-1726 and Chapter 20 of the Administrative Code of 
the City of Los Angeles. It is composed of six membership tiers with over 27,000 active 
and retired members, including survivors. The Plan also consists of a pensioner health 
subsidy plan operated under the provisions of the City of Los Angeles Charter (Sections 
1330, 1428, 1518, 1618, and 1718) and Administrative Code (Chapter 11.5 and Section 
4.2018 of Chapter 20). 
Plan net assets are valued at approximately $33 billion as of December 31, 2024 
(unaudited) and are invested in a diversified portfolio that includes public equities, fixed 
income, private equity, private credit, real estate, and private infrastructure. Total 
pension and health benefit expenses for fiscal year 2024-25 are projected to be $1.66 
billion. 
On July 1, 2022, LAFPP established an Internal Revenue Code Section 115 Trust Fund 
account for other post-employment benefits. The Section 115 Trust is presented as part 
of LAFPP’s financial statements for its Pension & Health Subsidy Plans and notes to the 
financial statements. As of June 2024, no separate opinion has been issued.
1.3 Prior Audits and Actuarial Reports 
The accounting firm Simpson & Simpson, LLP performed the audits of the financial 
statements for the fiscal years ending June 30, 2021 - 2024. The Segal Company, 
LAFPP’s consulting actuary, prepared the annual actuarial valuation of the Plan assets 
and liabilities as of June 30, 2024. The Plan's Audited Financial Statements and Annual 
Actuarial Valuations are available at https://lafpp.lacity.gov/about/inside-lafpp/financial-reports.

1.5 Scope of Services
The selected certified public accounting firm will be contracted to provide the following 
financial audit and advisory services:
1.5.1 Perform annual audits with the objective of expressing an opinion of whether 
LAFPP’s financial statements for its Pension & Health Subsidy Plans and notes 
to the financial statements are fairly presented, in all material aspects, in 
conformity with the Minimum Audit Requirements and Reporting Guidelines of 
Public Retirement Systems issued by the State of California Office of the 
Controller and the Government Auditing Standards issued by the Comptroller 
General of the United States.
As part of the scope of this service, the selected firm shall:
1. Submit independent audit reports expressing an opinion on LAFPP’s 
financial statements and notes to the financial statements. 
2. Communicate audit observations (whether caused by error or fraud) 
during the course of the audit in accordance with the Statements on 
Auditing Standards issued by the American Institute of Certified Public 
Accountants.
3. Test an appropriate sample of: (1) active member accounts for verification 
of service credit and total on deposit, (2) benefits granted during the year 
for service retirements, disabilities, survivorships, deferred retirement 
option plan (DROP) payments, medical and dental subsidies and 
reimbursements, and refunds of contributions. The auditor shall make an 
independent review of the procedures and payments and report the 
findings. 
4. Test census data provided to the actuary for completeness and accuracy. 
Select sample records of the active, inactive, and retired participants and

accumulation of reported cost, book value, and market value of the

1.18 CONTRACTOR DISCLOSURE POLICY 
A. PURPOSE 
It is the policy of LAFPP for Contractors to disclose conflicts of interest - actual, potential and 
perceived.
The goal of this Policy is to prevent impropriety or the appearance of impropriety, to provide 
transparency and confidence in LAFPP’s decision-making process, and to help ensure that 
investment and procurement decisions are made solely on the merits of the goods or services 
that Contractors propose to provide to LAFPP. 
This Policy sets forth the circumstances under which LAFPP requires the full and timely disclosure 
of ex parte communications with, relationships with, and payments to, entities such as placement 
agents, third party marketers, lobbyists and other Intermediaries. This Policy is intended to apply 
broadly to all Contractors with whom LAFPP conducts or potentially will conduct business. 
This Policy shall apply in addition to, and is intended to supplement, any applicable state and City 
ethics, campaign finance, and lobbying laws found in the City’s Charter, Governmental Ethics, 
Lobbying and Campaign Finance Ordinances, the California Political Reform Act, and the 
California Constitution. Unless otherwise specified or required by the context, all terms used but 
not defined herein shall have the same meanings ascribed to them in Appendix A. 
The Board recognizes that the flow of communication through staff between Contractors or 
consultants and Board members is beneficial to the conduct of system business. However, there 
are instances wherein Contractors or consultants may have ex parte communications directly with 
Board members. In those instances where the communications reasonably might give the 
appearance of being an attempt to influence the outcome of a Board or staff decision or consultant 
recommendation, the Board recognizes that there might be the potential for misunderstanding, 
misinformation, or conflicting instructions, and therefore the communications reasonably could be 
interpreted as inappropriately affecting the Board, staff, or consultant. Such communications do 
not always rise to the level of “undue influence” as defined in this policy, but nevertheless are 
subject to disclosure.

Contractors that are entered into after July 23, 2009, the date this Policy is adopted.
