ADMINISTRATIVE MATTERS

Type of Entity
The City of Austin Employees’ Retirement System (COAERS) is a single employer governmental
defined benefit retirement system originally established in 1941 by City ordinance and governed
since 1991 by Texas statute (Vernon’s Ann.Texas Civ.St. Art. 6243n). COAERS is qualified under
Section 401(a) of the Internal Revenue Code.

   COAERS Board of Trustees
   The COAERS Board of Trustees is vested with the statutory power and responsibility for the
   proper and effective general administration, management, and operation of the retirement
   system for the exclusive benefit of its present and future members and beneficiaries. The Board
   consists of 11 members comprised of City of Austin officials, employees, retirees, and citizens.

   Participating Employers
   Two employers participate in the Plan, the City of Austin Texas with over 17,000 active and
   deferred members and 7,100 retirees and the City of Austin Employees Retirement System
   staff with 38 active and deferred members and 13 retirees.

   Authority for Actuarial Services
   The COAERS Board of Trustees has statutory authority for the selection of an actuary. The
   statute states the Board of Trustees shall designate an actuary who shall be the technical
   advisor of the retirement board regarding the maintenance and operations of the fund but
   not an investment advisor or fiduciary with respect to any investments of the fund. The
   statute directs the actuary to perform valuations of the assets and liabilities of the fund.

   Actuarial Determine Contribution Plan
   COAERS is an actuarial determined contribution (ADC) rate plan in which, each year, the
   actuary determines the normal cost of the plan as a percentage of pay adjusting for the
   administrative expense and any liability layers established after December 31, 2022. The
   total normal cost of the plan is then forecasted for the upcoming year. The forecasted normal
   cost is reduced by the employee contribution rate to yield the upcoming years’ employer
   contribution rate. The statutory requirements for the plan, including the determination of
   the ADC, can be found in the COAERS statutes at TX CIV ST Art 6243n.

SECTION ONE - FUNDING APPROACH

1.1 Funding Approach in General
COAERS is an actuarial determined contribution (ADC) rate plan in which, each year, the actuary
determines the normal cost of the plan as a percentage of pay adjusting for the administrative
expense and amortization of any gain/loss layers (“liability layers) established after December
31, 2022. The total normal cost of the plan is then forecasted for the upcoming year. The
forecasted normal cost is reduced by the employee contribution rate to yield the upcoming years’
employer contribution rate. An existing amortization schedule of fixed payments, called Legacy
Liability payments, has also been created to retire the plan’s Unfunded Actuarial Accrued
Liability. The plan sponsor is required by law to make the required Legacy Liability payments in
addition to the employer’s contribution each year.

COAERS’ strategic funding objective is to meet the long-term pension obligations promised to
all members of the plan. To meet this objective, COAERS has put in place a set of policies and
legislative requirements to:

   1. Establish a fixed payment schedule to pay off the Unfunded Actuarial Accrued Liability
      (UAAL) over a fixed, closed 30-year period, known as the Legacy Liability.
   2. Conduct annual actuarial valuations, which include a Risk Sharing Valuation Study
      (RSVS), to determine the ADC rate for the upcoming year for the employers participating
      in the plan (See TX CIV ST Art. 6243n for a full description of the state statute detailing
      the Risk Sharing Valuation Study).
   3. Monitor the employer’s contribution rate relative to the expected future trajectory of
      the rate, known as a corridor midpoint.
   4. If the employer portion of the ADC exceeds the corridor maximum (5% of pay above the
      corridor midpoint), take action to increase employee contributions up to the 2%
      maximum, and discuss future changes to the Plan if the ADC exceeds the employer and
      employee maximums.
   5. Periodically conduct Actuarial Experience Studies to review and adopt the assumptions
      and methods used to determine the cost of the promised benefits. State law requires
      Experience Studies to perform no less than every five years. The last Experience Study
      was conducted as of December 31, 2024.

1.2 Actuarial Determined Contribution
COAERS is an actuarially determined contribution (ADC) rate plan. The ADC is comprised of the
normal cost and the administrative expense assumption adjusted for the amortization of any
liability layers expressed as a percentage of pay. COAERS uses the Entry Age Normal actuarial
cost method to develop the normal cost for each benefit component – retirement, termination,
disability, etc. The administrative expense assumption is currently .70% of pay.

In determining the future contribution rate, the actuary also determines if any adjustments are
needed for gains or losses by way of additional liability layers. Such layers have already been
established for 2023 and 2024 and will continue to be calculated each subsequent year. Future
                                               9
actuarial losses are converted to a percentage of payroll, amortized, and the layer added to the
normal cost. Future actuarial gains are converted to a percentage of payroll, amortized, and the
layer subtracted from the normal cost. Based upon the result, the actuary then projects the
normal cost for the future upcoming calendar year, and the employee contribution rate is
subtracted to yield the employer contribution rate for the upcoming year.

In addition, a model of future expected employer contribution rates has also been established to
help COAERS determine a range of future acceptable employer contribution rates. An Initial Risk
Sharing Valuation Study (RSVS) was performed as of December 31, 2022 establishing a
contribution rate corridor. The corridor has a midpoint which is the expected future trajectory
of the employer contribution rate. Additionally, a minimum and a maximum employer
contribution rate were also set at 5% of pay above and 5% of pay below the midpoint. This
corridor provides COAERS with a range of possible future employer contribution rates. The
contribution corridor is used to trigger important subsequent events. First, if the ADC would
cause the employer contribution rate to exceed the corridor’s maximum contribution rate, then
the employee contribution rate will have to increase accordingly. The increase would be added
to the 10% current employee contribution rate up to an additional 2% of payroll to cover the
shortfall. Second, if ADC exceeds the additional 2% of pay from the employees, then COAERS
and the City of Austin must enter into discussions to come up with a plan to address the funding
and the expense of the Plan. Finally, if the ADC is lower than the corridor midpoint, the employer
contribution will remain at the corridor midpoint so long as the funded ratio is below 90%.

1.5 Normal Cost Layers for Gains/Losses After 2022
Gains and losses realized after 2022, create new amortization layers adjusting the normal cost
and have the same amortization period as the Legacy Liability until the remaining period for the
Legacy Liability is less than 20 years. When the remaining amortization period of the Legacy
Liability is less than 20 years, new layers will be amortized over a closed 20-year period.

1.6 Legacy Liability Payment Schedule
The RSVS performed as of December 31, 2022 created a Legacy Liability amortization schedule
which establishes the payments the City of Austin is to contribute in addition to the ADC each
year to retire the unfunded actuarial accrued liability. The entire schedule of Legacy Liability
payments can be found at www.coaers.org/RFP.

1.8 Experience Studies
COAERS believes that a periodic review of our actuarial assumptions is crucial to understanding
and managing the cost of the benefits promised to members of COAERS. The use of outdated
or inappropriate assumptions can result in understated costs which will lead to higher future
contribution rates; or overstated costs which place an unnecessary financial burden on the City
of Austin and its employees. Accordingly, COAERS conducts experience studies every five years.
During each experience study, each assumption is reviewed and compared to the actual
experience of the plan with consideration of adding in a slight margin for adverse experience.
Then the assumptions, as a whole, are reviewed for reasonableness and consistency. COAERS
recently reviewed its actuarial assumptions based on census data as of December 31, 2024.
Certain key assumptions are listed below. A full discussion of the assumptions can be found in
the Experience Study Report at www.coaers/RFP.
