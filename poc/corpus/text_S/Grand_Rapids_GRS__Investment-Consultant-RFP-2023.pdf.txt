The City of Grand General Retirement System and the City of Grand Rapids Police and Fire
Retirement System (collectively the “Plan”) are requesting proposals for Investment Consulting
Services by 4:00 P.M. on Thursday, August 31, 2023.

I.     OVERVIEW

       The Boards of Trustees of the City of Grand Rapids General Retirement System and City
       of Grand Rapids Police and Fire Retirement System (collectively the “Board”) are in the
       process of reviewing their investment consultant services. The purpose of this RFP is to
       gain adequate information from which the Boards may evaluate such services. This RFP
       includes detail on general (non-discretionary) and discretionary (OCIO) services. Firms are
       asked to submit proposals for either, or both, as appropriate.

       Further information about the Plan is available at https://grpensions.org/.

       City of Grand Rapids General Retirement System

       The City of Grand Rapids General Retirement System has a total market value of
       approximately $437 Million as of June 30, 2023.

       The Plan’s portfolio consists of approximately: 24% fixed income; 17% U.S. equity; 10%
       global equity; 8% non-U.S. equity; 8% ADRs; 8% private equity; 5% treasury inflation-
       protected securities; 5% real estate (REITs); 5% commodities; 5% mid-stream energy; and
       5% private credit.

       City of Grand Rapids Police and Fire Retirement System

       The City of Grand Rapids Police and Fire Retirement System has a total market value of
       approximately $479 Million as of June 30, 2023.

       The Plan’s portfolio consists of approximately: 23% fixed income; 19% U.S. equity; 10%
       global equity; 8% non-U.S. equity; 9% ADRs; 7% private equity; 5% treasury inflation-
       protected securities; 5% real estate (REITs); 5% commodities; 5% mid-stream energy; and
       4% private credit.

       Northern Trust currently serves as custodian for the Plan’s assets.
A.   SCOPE OF SERVICES

     The primary role of the Investment Consultant is to provide objective, third-party
     advice and counsel that will enable the Board to make well-informed and well-
     educated decisions regarding the investment of Plan assets.

     Contractual services for investment consulting will include, but not be limited to,
     the following:

     1.     Development Investment Policy, Objectives and Guidelines

            Assist the Board in the development and periodic review of a policy
            statement that properly reflects the Board’s tolerance for risk and that best
            helps the Board meet its rate-of-return, funded status and administrative
            expense objectives.

     2.     Asset Allocation Studies

            Conduct periodic asset allocation studies to determine whether or not the
            current asset allocation falls within the Board's investment objectives and
            guidelines.

     3.     Investment Manager Search

            When deemed necessary, assist the Board in its due diligence and search for
            new investment manager(s) utilizing the appropriate data base.

     4.     Development of Investment Manager Performance
            Standards/Guidelines

            Assist the Board in the development and review of performance standards
            and guidelines with which the Board can measure each investment
            manager's progress. Attend monthly meetings or special meetings as
            requested by the Board.

     5.     General Consulting Services

            Provide general consulting services as requested by the Board. These might
            include custodial search and selection, etc.

     6.     Performance Measurement & Monitoring

            Monitor the performance of the investment manager(s) to provide the Board
            with the ability to determine the manager’s progress toward achieving the
            Board’s investment objectives. Prepare and provide written quarterly
            reports submitted to the Board.


                                      2
     7.    Monitor compliance with Act 314

           Monitor the investment of the Plan’s assets consistent with the limitations
           under Public Act 314 of 1965, as amended, with written quarterly reports
           submitted to the Board.

     8.    Assist in Fund Diversification/Asset Management

           Assist the Board in the development of an investment manager structure
           that provides adequate diversification with respect to the number and types
           of investment managers to be retained by the Board.

     9.    Historical Information Related to Specific Holdings

           Upon request, provide information regarding the holdings, sale and
           purchase of specific equities in order to complete proof of claims related to
           securities litigation.

     10.   Discretionary Investment Management Services (OPTIONAL)

           Fully discretionary investment management services, should the boards opt
           to enter into an OCIO agreement.

B.   MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS

     1.    The Investment Consultant must have a minimum of five (5) years of
           experience in providing institutional investor consulting services.

     2.    The Investment Consultant must provide services to at least one (1) public
           employee retirement system with at least $250 million in assets.

     3.    The Investment Consultant must be a registered investment advisor under
           the Investment Advisors Act of 1940.

     4.    The Investment Consultant shall acknowledge its responsibilities as an
           investment fiduciary under Public Act 314 of 1965, as amended.

     5.    If you are affiliating or partnering with a national firm, or are a local office
           of a national firm, the minimum qualification requirements herein must be
           satisfied by the individual or entity submitting this proposal.

     6.    The Investment Consultant must attend regular meetings, as well as special
           meetings upon request by the Board, at no additional cost to the Plan.

           The Board currently meets nine times per year.
