                   SHREWSBURY CONTRIBUTORY RETIREMENT SYSTEM

                    Request for Proposals for Investment Consultant Services
The SHREWSBURY RETIREMENT BOARD, overseers of the Shrewsbury Retirement System, a
Massachusetts municipal public pension find with assets of approximately $125 million, invites qualified
entities to submit proposals for Investment Consultant Services. The Board is subject to the oversight and
regulations of the Public Employment Retirement Administration Commission (“commission” or
“PERAC”.) and governed by Chapter 32 of Massachusetts General Laws (“Chapter 32”).The Board is
currently fully invested in the Pension Reserves Investment Trust (“PRIP’) Fund.
The Board is governed by a five member Board and is comprised of two units, namely the Town of
Shrewsbury and the Shrewsbury Housing Authority. The Board employs a staff of one person its office,
located in Shrewsbury Massachusetts.
The System currently has 493 Active Members, 153 Inactive Members, and 276 Retirees. As of January
1,2017, the System’s funding ratio was 86.6%.
It is expected that the chosen consultant will be retained for a period of five (5) years and not to exceed
seven (7) years, including any renewal or extension or option. The Board reserves the right to terminate
the contract executed between the Board and the selected proposer for any reason on thirty (30) days
written notice.
 It shall be the primary purpose of the consultant to advise the Board and monitor the portfolio and
investment managers, assist in asset allocation models, attend Shrewsbury Retirement Board monthly
meetings upon request, prepare portfolio performance reports and interface with regulatory personnel.

                            EVALUATION CRITERIA AND RATING
The Board will select the most advantageous proposal based on the ratings and written evaluation of the
Board and the cost proposals. The most advantageous proposal may not be the lowest cost proposal and it
may not have the highest composite ranking. The selected proposal will be based on the Board’s
evaluation of the costs and benefits associated with each proposal. The proposal selected will be that
which provides the best combination of quality and price for the System.
The proposals submitted in response to the RFP will be considered separately, opened separately and
evaluated separately.
1. Excepting “minor informalities,” proposals for services not addressing or meeting the Minimum
   Criteria will not be considered “responsive” and will be excluded from further evaluation. A “minor
   informality” is defined as: minor deviations, insignificant mistakes, and matters of form rather than
   substance of the bid, proposal, or contact document which can be waived or corrected without
   prejudice to other bidders, potential bidders, or the governmental body.
